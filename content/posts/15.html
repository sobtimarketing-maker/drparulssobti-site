Sometimes the thing that bothers you about your smile is tiny. One front tooth is a touch longer than the other. A canine has a pointy tip that catches your eye in photos. An edge has a little notch in it. For small things like these, the answer can be to take away a fraction of a millimetre of enamel rather than add anything at all.

What contouring is

Enamel contouring, also called enameloplasty or recontouring, means gently reshaping the outer surface of a tooth. We use fine polishing discs and burs to smooth a rough edge, round off a sharp corner or bring a slightly long tooth level with its neighbour. Then we polish the surface until it's smooth and glossy again.

The amount removed is very small, usually well under a millimetre and always within the enamel layer. Enamel has no nerves, so most people don't need an injection. You'll feel some vibration and hear the instrument, and that's about it.

What it's good for

Contouring works for small irregularities in shape. Uneven biting edges, one tooth slightly longer than the other, small chips, little bumps or ridges on the surface and pointed canines that look a bit sharp. It's also useful for slight overlaps, where taking a sliver off one edge makes two teeth look straighter.

It often works best alongside something else. A bit of contouring after braces or aligners can tidy up the edges once the teeth are straight. Contouring one tooth and bonding its neighbour can balance a smile that neither treatment would fix on its own.

What it can't do

Since contouring only takes away, it can't make a tooth longer, close a gap or change colour. It also has limits. Enamel doesn't grow back, so we're careful about how much we remove, and teeth with thin enamel aren't good candidates. If a tooth is quite crooked or the change you want is large, removing enamel would go too deep, and alignment or bonding is the better route.

We always check the bite first, too. The edges of your front teeth help guide how your jaw moves, so changing their shape without thinking about function can cause problems later.

Seeing it before we start

Because the change is permanent, we like to show you first. We can mark the planned shape on a photograph or a digital design, and for front teeth we sometimes check it with a quick mock-up so you can see the effect in the mirror. Only once you're happy do we pick up the polishing discs.

The treatment itself is quick, usually done at a single visit. Some people notice slight sensitivity for a day or so, but it generally settles on its own.
