Almost everyone's teeth darken a little over the years. Some of that is stain sitting on the surface, and some is a change inside the tooth itself. Knowing which is which decides what will actually work.

Two kinds of stain

Dentists sort discolouration into two groups:

  • Extrinsic stains sit on the outer surface of the enamel and in the thin film that forms on teeth through the day.
  • Intrinsic stains lie inside the tooth, in the enamel or the dentine beneath it.

Many people have a mix of both.

Surface stains: the everyday culprits

  • Tea and coffee. Both are rich in tannins, which cling to the enamel surface. Strong, milk-free tea is one of the most common causes of brown stain we see.
  • Tobacco and paan. Smoking, chewing tobacco, gutka and paan leave stubborn brown to black stains, and they also harm the gums.
  • Coloured foods and drinks. Red wine, cola, turmeric-rich curries, beetroot and berries can all add colour over time.
  • Some mouthwashes. Chlorhexidine rinses, often prescribed for short periods after gum treatment, can leave a brown film if used for weeks.
  • Plaque and tartar. Rough deposits near the gum line pick up stain easily.

Surface stains usually respond well to a professional clean and polish. Regular cleans help keep them away.

Deep stains: changes inside the tooth

  • Age. Enamel slowly thins with wear, so more of the yellow dentine shows through. The dentine itself also becomes thicker and darker with time.
  • Genetics. Some people are simply born with darker or more yellow teeth, or with thinner enamel.
  • Medicines in childhood. Tetracycline antibiotics taken while the teeth were forming can leave grey or brown bands.
  • Too much fluoride while teeth form. Fluorosis can cause white flecks or, in more severe cases, brown patches. It is common in parts of India where groundwater is high in fluoride.
  • Injury or a dead nerve. A single tooth that turns grey or dark after a knock often has a damaged nerve and needs checking, not just whitening.
  • Old fillings and decay. Ageing tooth-coloured fillings pick up stain, old metal fillings can make a tooth look grey, and decay shows as brown or chalky patches.
Before any whitening plan, we find out why a tooth is dark. The cause decides the cure.

Why the cause matters

A tooth darkened by tea will brighten with cleaning and whitening. A tooth darkened by tetracycline may lighten only partly and might be better served by bonding or veneers later. A single dark tooth after an injury may need root canal treatment first, and sometimes internal whitening from inside the tooth. Decay must be treated before anything cosmetic. Getting the diagnosis right avoids spending money on a treatment that cannot do the job.
