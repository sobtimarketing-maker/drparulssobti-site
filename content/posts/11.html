This is where the second chapter of the series begins, with small changes that can make a big difference. And the smallest of them is one many people skip for years: a proper professional clean. It's quick and rarely painful, and the change in colour alone often surprises people.

What scaling actually removes

Even if you brush well, a soft film called plaque builds up on your teeth every day. Whatever plaque you miss hardens within a few days into tartar, also called calculus. Once it's hard, no toothbrush will shift it. It collects mostly along the gum line, behind the lower front teeth and on the cheek side of the upper back teeth, near where the salivary glands open.

Tartar is rough and porous, so it soaks up stain from tea, coffee and tobacco and turns yellow, brown or even black. It also keeps the gums irritated. Most of the bleeding people notice when they brush comes from gums that are reacting to plaque and tartar sitting against them.

Scaling is simply the removal of that hard deposit, above the gum line and just below it.

What happens in the chair

Most of the work is done with an ultrasonic scaler. It's a fine tip that vibrates very fast and breaks the tartar off the tooth while a gentle spray of water washes it away and keeps things cool. You'll hear a high-pitched buzz and feel some vibration and water. Small hand instruments are then used to clean the tighter spots and check that the surfaces are smooth.

After that comes polishing. A soft rubber cup and a mildly gritty paste take off the surface stain that's left and leave the teeth smooth, which also makes it harder for new plaque to stick. For a typical clean the whole thing is done in a single visit.

Does it hurt?

For most people it's more of an odd feeling than pain. If your gums are very inflamed or you have sensitive teeth, some spots may be uncomfortable, and we can numb those areas if needed. Just tell us. Gums may bleed a little during the clean and feel slightly tender for a day or two afterwards. That settles quickly as they heal.

A lot of people have heard that scaling loosens teeth or makes gaps appear. It doesn't. What can happen is that tartar has been bridging the space between teeth, sometimes for years, and once it's gone you can feel the gap the gum disease had already made underneath. Leaving the tartar there would only let that get worse.

Why it comes before everything else

Whitening works better on clean teeth, because the gel reaches the enamel instead of sitting on deposits. Shades for crowns and veneers are only reliable once the stain is off. And as we wrote in the post on healthy gums, nothing cosmetic should go onto a mouth with inflamed gums.

There's a simpler benefit too. Quite often, once the stain and tartar are gone, people decide their teeth are whiter than they thought and don't need anything more.
