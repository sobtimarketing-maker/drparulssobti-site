Veneers have a reputation problem. Mention them and many people picture the very white, very even teeth of a film star, the kind you can spot from across a room. That's one way to use them, but it isn't really what they're for. This chapter is about porcelain and composite work, and it makes sense to start with what veneers actually do.

What a veneer is

A veneer is a thin shell bonded to the front surface of a tooth. It covers the part you see when you smile and leaves the back and most of the tooth underneath as they are. Think of it as a new face for the tooth rather than a replacement for it.

They come in two broad kinds. Composite veneers are built up directly on the tooth in the chair, using the same material as bonding. Ceramic or porcelain veneers are made to measure in a lab and then fixed on. The next post compares the two properly, so here we'll stick to what either kind can do.

The problems veneers solve

Colour is the most common reason. Some teeth don't whiten well, such as those with grey tetracycline banding, or a single front tooth that darkened after an old injury. A veneer covers the colour instead of trying to bleach it out.

Shape is the next. Teeth that are worn short, chipped, pointed or simply small for the face can be given a new outline. Peg-shaped lateral incisors, which are much narrower than normal, are a classic case. So are front teeth that have been ground down over years of clenching, once the grinding itself is under control.

Veneers can also close small gaps and make slightly crooked teeth look straight. We say "look" on purpose. The tooth underneath hasn't moved, so this only works when the crowding is mild. If teeth are noticeably out of line, aligning them first usually gives a better result and means less of the tooth has to be shaped.

Who they suit

The best candidates have healthy gums and no active decay. There also needs to be enough enamel left for the veneer to bond to strongly. A stable bite matters too. Veneers on teeth that take heavy force, especially in people who grind, are at more risk of chipping or coming loose unless that's managed with a night guard.

They're less suited to teeth that are already heavily filled or broken down, where a crown often protects the tooth better. And they're not a fix for teeth that are badly misaligned or for a bite that doesn't meet properly.

What a good result looks like

Done well, veneers shouldn't announce themselves. The colour should suit your skin and the whites of your eyes. Natural teeth also differ slightly from one another in shape, and the edges let a little light through, so good veneers copy that instead of looking like a uniform row.
