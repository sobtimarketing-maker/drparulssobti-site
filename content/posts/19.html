Nothing we do to teeth lasts forever, and anyone who tells you otherwise is overselling. What we can say is roughly how long each kind of work tends to hold up, what shortens its life, and what keeps it looking good for longer. Most of that last part is in your hands rather than ours.

Whitening

Whitening fades gradually rather than all at once. How quickly depends mostly on what you eat and drink. Someone who has four cups of chai a day, or chews paan, will see colour creep back sooner than someone who mostly drinks water. For many people the result stays pleasing for a year or two, sometimes longer, before they feel like a top-up.

If you had take-home trays made, keep them. A few nights of gel every so often is usually enough to bring the shade back, and it costs far less than starting again. Rinsing with water after tea or coffee helps more than people expect.

Composite bonding

Bonding usually lasts several years before it needs attention, and with care it can go a good deal longer. The material slowly loses its shine and picks up surface stain. Edges can chip, especially on front teeth that take a lot of biting force.

The nice thing about composite is that it's easy to look after. At your regular cleaning we can polish it back to a gloss, and a small chip can often be repaired by adding a little material rather than redoing the whole tooth. So the "lifespan" of bonding is really the time until it needs a touch-up.

Veneers and crowns

Porcelain is harder and much more stain-resistant than composite, and well-made ceramic veneers and crowns often last ten years or more. The porcelain itself rarely wears out. What usually ends a veneer's life is something around it: the gum shrinking back and showing an edge, decay starting at the margin, or a crack from grinding or biting something hard.

That's why the check-ups matter so much once you have them. We're watching the edges and the gums, not just the porcelain.

Polished, healthy gums

A scaling and polish is the one result that fades fastest. Tartar starts rebuilding within weeks if brushing and flossing slip. Most people do well with a professional clean every six months, and some need it more often. It sounds like a minor thing, but it's what keeps every other piece of work looking good.

Habits that cost you

A few things come up again and again when cosmetic work fails early. Grinding or clenching at night is the big one, and many people don't know they do it until a partner mentions the noise or we spot the wear. A night guard protects both natural teeth and anything we've added. Opening bottles or packets with your teeth, biting nails, chewing pens and cracking supari or hard nuts all put sideways force on edges that aren't built for it.

Smoking stains bonding and the margins of veneers, and it harms the gums that hold everything in place.

What a realistic plan looks like

When we plan a smile with you, we'll tell you what upkeep it will need, including how often to come in and whether a night guard makes sense. We'd rather you know at the start that bonding may need polishing every year or two than be surprised later. Good care won't make anything permanent, but it does decide whether a result lasts a few years or a great many.
