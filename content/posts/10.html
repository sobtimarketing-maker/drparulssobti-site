We've spent nine posts on teeth, gums and proportions. This one is about the place where all of it happens. If you've never been to Dr Sobti's Dental Care, this is roughly what an ordinary working day there looks like.

Before the first patient

The clinic opens at 10 in the morning, seven days a week, and closes at 7 in the evening. Before anyone sits in a chair, the day's instruments have already been through sterilisation. Every instrument is cleaned, sealed in a pouch and sterilised in a Class B autoclave, the hospital-grade kind, for every single patient. Gloves, masks, needles, suction tips and bibs are used once and thrown away, and the chair and surfaces are wiped down between each appointment. Patients rarely see any of this, and it's the part of the day we're strictest about.

A mix of appointments

Dr Parul's list might start with a routine check-up and clean, then a child's first visit, then someone who has come in for a smile consultation and wants to talk through options. Later there might be a trial smile fitting, where a patient sees a mock-up of their new teeth in their own mouth for the first time. For a lot of people that's the moment the whole plan stops being a picture on a screen and starts feeling real.

In between, there are always a few unplanned visits: a broken tooth, a toothache that started the night before, a crown that came off over lunch. We do our best to see those quickly.

Working alongside Dr Pushkar

Dr Parul runs the clinic with Dr Pushkar Sobti, whose work is mainly dental implants and full-mouth rehabilitation. The clinic has a dedicated implant surgery suite alongside two general dentistry chairs, so on many days one of them is working on a smile design while the other is in surgery.

Their work overlaps a lot. Someone missing a front tooth may need an implant from Dr Pushkar and then a crown that Dr Parul designs to match the teeth beside it. Because both work from the same 3D scans and photographs, they can plan the whole thing together rather than passing a patient from one clinic to another.

Work that happens away from the chair

A good part of the day isn't in the chair at all. Smile designs are planned on screen, using photographs and scans of the patient's face and teeth. There's a CBCT scanner for 3D X-rays, intraoral scanners in place of messy impressions, design software and an in-house lab with milling machines and a 3D printer. That means many things, from trial smiles to some crowns, can be designed and made under one roof instead of being sent away and waited on.

The team

The clinic has a team of twelve people, from the front desk to the dental assistants and lab. They're the ones who call to remind you about your appointment, sterilise the instruments, hold your hand if you're nervous and make sure the next patient isn't kept waiting.

If you're nearby, in Rajouri Garden or elsewhere in West Delhi, you're welcome to drop in and see it for yourself.
