Most people come to a dentist with a single complaint: a chipped edge, a yellow tooth, a gap they have hidden for years. Smile designing starts somewhere else. It starts with your face.

A smile is more than teeth

When you smile, four things move together: your teeth, your gums, your lips and the muscles of your face. A smile can have perfectly white teeth and still look wrong if the gum line is uneven, if the teeth are too long for the lips, or if the centre of the smile does not line up with the centre of the face.

Smile designing is the planning process that looks at all four together. It asks what a smile should look like on this face, at this age, for this person, before anyone picks up an instrument.

What a smile design looks at

  • Facial midline. The line through the centre of your forehead, nose and chin. The gap between your two upper front teeth should sit close to it.
  • Smile line. The curve made by the edges of your upper teeth. In most pleasing smiles it follows the curve of the lower lip.
  • Tooth proportions. The width and length of each front tooth, and how they relate to each other. We will spend a whole post on this.
  • Gum line. How much gum shows, and whether it is level from side to side.
  • Colour. Not just how white the teeth are, but whether that shade suits your skin, eyes and age.
  • Function. How your teeth meet when you bite. A beautiful smile that does not bite properly will not last.

Who smile designing is for

It is not only for big makeovers. A single crown on a front tooth benefits from the same planning, because that crown has to match its neighbours in shape, shade and position. The difference is scale. For one tooth the plan might take ten minutes. For a full smile it can take several visits, photographs and a trial smile you can see in your own mouth.

The best result is one people notice without being able to say what changed. They simply say you look well.
