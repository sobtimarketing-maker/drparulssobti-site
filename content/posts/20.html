If you've read this far in the series, you may have noticed a pattern. Before veneers we talked about whitening. Before crowns, bonding. Before bonding, a simple polish or a little contouring. That order is deliberate, and it comes from the way Dr Parul has worked since she started in practice in 2009.

Enamel is the one thing we can't give back

Every treatment sits somewhere on a scale. At one end are things that leave the tooth exactly as it was, like cleaning and whitening, and most bonding comes close. At the other end are things that permanently remove tooth structure, like preparing a tooth for a veneer or a crown. Once enamel is drilled away it doesn't grow back, and the tooth will need some kind of covering for the rest of your life.

So the question Dr Parul asks at the start is a simple one. What is the smallest change that will give this person the smile they want? Sometimes the answer really is porcelain. Quite often it's something much lighter.

What that looks like in practice

Say someone comes in wanting veneers on their front six teeth because they look yellow and a little uneven. Before planning veneers, we'd check whether whitening would deal with the colour on its own. If two teeth are slightly crooked, short aligner treatment might straighten them so nothing needs to be shaped down. A chipped edge might take a small bit of bonding. Put together, those steps can give a result the patient is happy with while their own teeth stay intact.

It doesn't always work out that way. If teeth are heavily filled or badly worn, lighter options won't get there, and pretending otherwise would waste your time and money. Then porcelain is the right answer, and we'll say so. Even then the aim is to prepare the teeth as little as the design allows, which digital planning helps with, because we know before we start exactly how much room each veneer needs.

Why she works this way

Part of it is about the long run. A thirty-year-old with veneers will probably need them replaced at some point, maybe more than once, and each replacement can take a little more tooth. Starting conservatively keeps more options open for later.

The other reason is that the gentle options have become very good. Modern bonding and clear aligners can now do things that once needed crowns, and it would be a shame not to use them. People also tend to relax when the first suggestion isn't the most expensive one, which makes it easier for them to ask questions and say what they actually want.

What you can expect at a consultation

When you come in, we'll usually show you more than one route. There may be a lighter plan and a more complete one, with the trade-offs of each laid out plainly. You choose. If you'd like to start small and see how you feel, that's always fine, and nothing we do in the early steps rules out the bigger ones later.
