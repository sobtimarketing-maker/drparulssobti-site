Before a smile can be designed it has to be understood. Dentists read a smile in four layers, from the inside out: teeth, gums, lips and face.

Layer 1: the teeth

The six upper front teeth do most of the work in a smile. From the centre outwards they are the two central incisors, the two lateral incisors and the two canines. The centrals are the largest and set the tone. The laterals are narrower and slightly shorter, which softens the smile. The canines turn the corner, and how much of them shows decides whether a smile looks narrow or broad.

Each tooth has a shape (square, oval or tapering), a surface texture, and edges that may be straight, rounded or slightly worn. Younger smiles tend to have rounder edges and small gaps between the incisal corners. With age the edges flatten.

Layer 2: the gums

Gums frame the teeth the way a mount frames a picture. Healthy gums are firm, pale pink and scalloped, rising to a point between each tooth. The highest point of the gum on each tooth is called the zenith. On the centrals and canines it sits slightly towards the back of the tooth; on the laterals it is a little lower. When these heights are uneven, even perfect teeth can look crooked.

Layer 3: the lips

The lips decide how much of the teeth and gums are seen. At rest, a few millimetres of the upper front teeth usually show below the upper lip. This amount shrinks with age, which is one reason older smiles can look tired. When you smile fully, the upper lip rises; if it lifts well above the gum line, more gum shows than most people like.

Layer 4: the face

Finally the smile sits inside a face. Its centre should line up with the facial midline. Its curve should follow the lower lip. Its width should suit the width of the face. And the overall look should suit the person's age and character. A very square, very white smile can look striking on one face and artificial on another.

We design from the face inwards and treat from the inside out: health first, then teeth, then gums, then the lips and face if needed.

Why this order matters

Every treatment you will read about later in this series works on one or more of these layers. Whitening and veneers change the teeth. Laser contouring changes the gums. Fillers and anti-wrinkle injections change the lips and face. A good plan decides which layers need attention, and in what order, before starting any of them.
