A gap between the two front teeth is one of the most common things people ask us to fix. Some people like theirs and wouldn't change it. Others have been hiding it for years. If you're in the second group, the good news is there are several ways to close it, and the right one depends on why the gap is there.

First, why is there a gap?

A gap between the front teeth has a proper name, a diastema, and it can have several causes. Sometimes the teeth are simply a little small for the jaw. Sometimes one tooth, often a lateral incisor, is narrower than usual or missing. A thick band of tissue between the upper lip and gum, called the frenum, can hold the front teeth apart. Habits like thumb sucking or pushing the tongue against the teeth when swallowing can open gaps over time.

And in adults, a gap that has appeared or grown recently can be a sign of gum disease, with the teeth drifting as the bone around them weakens. That one needs treating first. Closing the gap cosmetically without fixing the gums would only hide a problem that keeps getting worse.

Option one: bonding

For a small gap, composite bonding is often the quickest answer. We add tooth-coloured material to the sides of the teeth next to the gap, shaped to match, usually in one visit with no drilling. It's the least costly option and usually reversible. The limit is size. Close a big gap with bonding alone and the front teeth end up looking too wide, so it suits gaps of a millimetre or two best.

Option two: veneers

If the teeth also need changes in colour or shape, ceramic veneers can close a gap and improve the whole smile at the same time. They're stronger and hold their colour better than composite, but they cost more and usually involve removing a thin layer of enamel. Like bonding, they can only close a gap so far before the teeth look out of proportion. We cover veneers properly in a later chapter, and you can see the costs on our veneers page.

Option three: moving the teeth

For larger gaps, or when there are gaps throughout the mouth, the best result usually comes from moving the teeth together with clear aligners or braces. This takes longer, but it keeps your natural teeth as they are and often fixes the bite at the same time. After the teeth are moved, a retainer is essential, because gaps have a habit of reopening.

Often the best plan is a mix. Aligners bring the teeth most of the way, and a little bonding at the end evens out the shapes.

What decides the price

The cost depends mostly on which route suits you and how many teeth are involved. Bonding one or two teeth costs far less than aligners or a set of veneers. The size of the gap matters, since a bigger gap may rule out the cheaper options. Any treatment needed first, such as gum care or fillings, adds to the total. Where a missing tooth is the reason for the gap, replacing it, perhaps with an implant, becomes part of the plan too.

We won't quote a price for gap closing without seeing you, because it really does vary. At the consultation we'll look at the cause, show you which options will work and give you a written estimate before you decide.
