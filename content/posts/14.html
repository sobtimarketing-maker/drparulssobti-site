You bit into something hard and a corner of your front tooth came away. Or there's a small gap between your two front teeth that's always bothered you. For problems like these, there's often no need for crowns or veneers. A tooth-coloured material can be shaped straight onto the tooth, usually in one sitting. That's composite bonding.

What composite is

Composite is a tooth-coloured filling material, a resin packed with very fine glass-like particles. It's soft and putty-like when it goes on, so it can be shaped by hand, and it sets hard in seconds under a blue curing light. It comes in many shades and levels of translucency, which is how a skilled dentist can layer it to look like natural enamel.

What it can fix

Bonding works well for small to moderate changes. Chipped or broken corners. Edges worn down by grinding. A small gap between the front teeth. A tooth that's a bit shorter or narrower than its neighbour. Small dark spots or white flecks on the surface. It's also used to cover the exposed root near the gum line when it's sensitive.

It's less suited to big changes in colour across several teeth, or to teeth that need a lot of rebuilding. Those are usually better handled with whitening, ceramic veneers or crowns, which we'll cover later in the series.

How it's done

We start by matching the shade, ideally after any whitening, since composite won't change colour later. Most bonding needs little or no drilling. The surface of the tooth is lightly roughened with a mild gel so the material can grip, then a bonding liquid is painted on and set with the light.

The composite then goes on in thin layers, each one shaped and set before the next. Once the shape is right, it's trimmed, checked against your bite and polished until it shines like the teeth beside it. Usually no injection is needed. For a chip or a small gap, it's generally done in a single visit.

For bigger bonding work across several front teeth, we often plan it first with a digital design and a mock-up, the same way we plan a full smile, so you can see the shape before we start.

The trade-offs

Bonding is quick, kind to the tooth and easy to repair or add to later. If you change your mind, it can usually be removed without having lost much of your natural tooth.

But composite isn't as tough as porcelain. It can chip if you bite thread or open packets with your teeth. It also picks up stain from tea, coffee and paan more easily than enamel does and slowly loses some of its shine. Most bonding lasts several years with good care, and a quick polish at your regular clean helps keep it looking fresh. When it does wear, it can usually be touched up rather than redone.

Looking after it

Treat bonded teeth like your own: brush them twice a day and keep up your check-ups. Avoid using them as tools. If you grind your teeth at night, a night guard will protect the bonding along with everything else.
